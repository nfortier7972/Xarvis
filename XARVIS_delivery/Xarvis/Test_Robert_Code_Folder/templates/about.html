The project

Since 2025, NASA's SPHEREx telescope has mapped the whole sky every six months in 102 colours of infrared light. Asteroids, comets, nearby brown dwarfs and perhaps undiscovered planets give themselves away by moving between those maps, but there is far too much data for anyone to search alone.

XARVIS is a public web tool that makes that search approachable. It shows where SPHEREx is and what it can see, lets anyone point at a patch of sky by its coordinates, and lines that patch up with the stars and asteroids known to be there, so that changes over time stand out. All positions come from official NASA, ESA and IPAC data.

Who we are

We are team XARVIS, competing in the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge from Calgary, Canada. Our interests span astrophysics, coding, data analysis and visualization, and design, and we work in English and French.
